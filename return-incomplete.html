Concordance · 2364
A body can be borrowed.
The damage still belongs to you.

Distance has become negotiable. Ownership has not.

Some parts of the past do not stay buried.

In 2364, Concordance technology can carry enough of a human mind across the Solar System for a distant Frame to feel like flesh. Governments built soldiers around it. Corporations built industries. Everyone eventually found a way to put a price on the body at the other end.

Thomas Rook was one of the soldiers. Once a Cinder, he fought through bodies waiting millions of kilometres from the flesh he was born in. Now the programme is gone, his own body carries the damage, and Rook makes a living recovering Frames for lenders in London.

Then a carefully chosen recovery job leads him to buried military hardware tied to an operation he cannot remember.

The system recognises him.

Rook does not recognise what it remembers.

Far from Earth, independent salvager Ada Mercer and pilot Julian Cross recover an abandoned military body from a vessel whose history has been deliberately erased. The Crown wants it back. Other people are willing to kill for it.

As their paths converge, Rook is forced towards a past he thought he had survived and towards a question the people who built him would rather leave unanswered:

What exactly came back?

Return Incomplete is the first novel in The Continuity Cycle, a far-future science-fiction series about memory, identity, loyalty and the ownership of the human self.
